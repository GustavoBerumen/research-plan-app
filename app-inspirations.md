# App inspirations

Tools that master "guided input," structured micro-coaching, and contextual scaffolding. These apps excel at transforming intimidating text boxes into approachable, step-by-step thinking experiences.

When non-researchers see a giant blank text box labeled "Research Questions" or "Problem Statement," they freeze or fill it with vague assumptions. The apps below solve this by breaking complex writing into bite-sized inputs, providing live contextual nudges, and embedding best-practice frameworks right where the user types.

## 1. Duolingo — Scaffolding & Micro-Steps

- **What it does well:** Duolingo never hands a beginner a blank canvas to "write an essay in Spanish." Instead, it uses scaffolding — fill-in-the-blanks, word banks, and immediate micro-feedback.
- **Inspiration for the Research Plan App:** When a user is drafting a Research Objective or Hypothesis, avoid giving them a blank text box right away. Give them a sentence-starter template with smart placeholders (e.g., "We believe that [User Segment] struggles with [Problem] because...") to lower the barrier to entry before they write freely.

## 2. Grammarly & Hemingway Editor — Inline Quality Indicators & Tone Meters

- **What they do well:** As you type in a text box, Grammarly doesn't just check spelling; it evaluates clarity, tone, and delivery in real-time, showing a score drawer on the right side.
- **Inspiration for the Research Plan App:** Adopt a "Plan Health & Clarity" gauge. As the user fills out the Problem Context or Methodology, an inline side panel evaluates whether the draft hits the 3 criteria from the app guide: Clarity, Actionability, and Feasibility.

## 3. Submittable & Typeform — One Question at a Time & Micro-Prompts

- **What they do well:** Submittable (used for grant applications) and Typeform break multi-step submissions into chunked, focused screens. They use in-field micro-copy (e.g., "Tip: Don't exceed 2 sentences. Focus on measurable impact.") directly above or below the text field.
- **Inspiration for the Research Plan App:** Implement Consciousness Prompts right inside the input field. Before the user types their Outcomes, show a micro-prompt like: "What concrete product decision will change based on this research?"

## 4. TurboTax / Taxfix — Socratic Wizard for Complex Legal/Financial Rules

- **What they do well:** Tax filing is notorious for forcing people to fill out forms they know nothing about. TurboTax turns hundreds of dense tax codes into a conversational Socratic interview: "Did you buy a home this year? Yes/No. Great, here is what that means for your return."
- **Inspiration for the Research Plan App:** Use a similar diagnostic wizard for selecting research methods. Instead of letting a user guess whether they need an Unmoderated Usability Test or Generative Interviews, ask them 2 quick Socratic questions: "Are you validating a concept or exploring a problem?" and "Do you have a working prototype ready?"

## 5. Linear & Notion — Block-Based Structure & Contextual Placeholders

- **What they do well:** Linear's issue creator and Notion's templates use rich-text blocks with detailed ghost-text placeholders. They don't just say "Description" — they outline the exact bullet points required for a good report.
- **Inspiration for the Research Plan App:** Map the Research Plan Template into modular, card-based blocks (e.g., Alignment, Project Context, Execution). Each block has ghost text showing a "Good vs. Bad" example that toggles off once the user begins typing.

## Key takeaway for the UI architecture

The best guided-input apps combine three distinct layers on screen:

1. **The Scaffold (Center):** Structured, chunked inputs with smart sentence starters.
2. **The Example Layer (Contextual):** Toggleable "Good vs. Bad" examples directly beneath the active text box.
3. **The Socratic Coach (Side Drawer):** Live feedback that evaluates clarity and asks probing questions without auto-completing the work.
