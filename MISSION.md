# Mission

**Status:** Accepted, 14 October 2026. Tracked in [RPA-168](https://turingtestable.atlassian.net/browse/RPA-168).

This file is the canonical statement of what the app is for. It is derived from
the Alignment statement in the team's working document, and where the two
disagree, **this file wins** — the Doc is a drafting surface. Decided by Gus,
13 October 2026.

## Purpose

To help someone turn what they already know about their project into a research
plan good enough to act on.

Three things that means in practice, in the order they were written down:

- **Eliminate non-essential questions.** A question that changes no decision
  should not be asked.
- **Resolve inefficient workflows.** Asking for the same thing twice, or asking
  for it before the answer can exist, is a workflow problem rather than a
  wording problem.
- **Deliver a more intuitive and effortless form.** Effortless is the measure.
  Not shorter for its own sake, and not more thorough for its own sake.

## The guiding principle

**Users know their project. They may have no experience writing a research
plan.**

Everything else follows from that asymmetry. The app does not need to supply
knowledge about the project — the person has it, and it cannot be obtained from
anywhere else. What it supplies is the shape of a plan, and enough coaching to
turn one into the other.

This is why the app asks rather than tells, and why its feedback is Socratic:
pointing out gaps and asking questions rather than rewriting an answer. A tool
that writes the plan for someone produces a document nobody owns.

## The four things the app does

1. **Collects** — structured sections and questions, so that a plan has a shape
   before it has content.
2. **Evaluates** — reads what has been written against a rubric and says what is
   weak, without blocking anyone from continuing.
3. **Refines** — offers to improve an answer, without taking the author's words
   away from them.
4. **Suggests** — proposes methods, frameworks and schedules from what the person
   has already said.

All four are support, not gatekeeping. None of them decides whether a plan is
finished.

## What the mission does not cover

**The app is the first step.** It produces a plan; the plan is then revised and
iterated, either in the app or in an editable document that leaves it.

This is the boundary, and it is here to be used. Work that belongs *after* a plan
exists — tracking whether the research happened, recording what was learned,
managing a study while it runs, reporting results — is outside this mission, no
matter how naturally it follows from a plan being written. A proposal can be a
good idea and still not be this app's job.

Two consequences worth stating, because they are the ones that get argued:

- **Being an application rather than a form** is about returning to revise a
  plan, which is inside the mission. It is not a licence to grow into a research
  management tool, which is outside it.
- **Signing a plan off** is the last thing the app does, not the first thing in a
  longer workflow it owns.

## How this file is used

A proposed change should be answerable against this file. If a feature does not
serve the purpose, respect the principle, or fall inside the boundary, that is an
argument against it that does not depend on anyone's taste.

Where this file and a decision record disagree about a specific decision, the
decision record is more specific and wins on that point — and the disagreement is
worth noticing, because one of the two is out of date.

  alternatives were rejected
- [`research/field-audit.md`](research/field-audit.md) — per-field verdicts, and
  the evidence for the October cut
