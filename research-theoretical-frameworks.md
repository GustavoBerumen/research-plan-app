# Theoretical Frameworks for UX Research & HCI

A curated collection of foundational theories, frameworks, and academic literature from Human-Computer Interaction (HCI), Cognitive Psychology, Sociology, and Pedagogy. This document serves as a knowledge reference for integrating rigorous theoretical lenses into user research planning, objective setting, and synthesis.

---

## 1. Social, Sociotechnical & Cultural Frameworks

### Practice Theory / Practice Perspective
* **Core Focus:** Treats everyday routines, habits, and collective social practices as the core unit of analysis, rather than focusing solely on individual attitudes, decision-making, or isolated system interactions.
* **UXR Application:** Ideal for evaluating how a new product, tool, or software workflow integrates into (or disrupts) existing organizational routines, household habits, or everyday socio-cultural practices.
* **Key References:**
  * Reckwitz, A. (2002). Toward a theory of social practices: A development in culturalist theorizing. *European Journal of Social Theory*, 5(2), 243–263. https://doi.org/10.1177/13684310222225432
  * Kuijer, L., de Jong, A., & van Eijk, D. (2013). Practices as a unit of design: An exploration of theoretical framework and design method. *ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction*, 20(4), 1–22. https://doi.org/10.1145/2509404.2509407

### Activity Theory
* **Core Focus:** Analyzes human activities as systemic, mediated interactions between a Subject, Object, Tools, Rules, Community, and Division of Labor.
* **UXR Application:** Exceptional for complex enterprise software, multi-role workplace ecosystems, and platforms where systemic breakdowns or contradictions occur between team roles, tools, and social rules.
* **Key References:**
  * Engeström, Y. (1987). *Learning by Expanding: An Activity-Theoretical Approach to Developmental Research*. Orienta-Konsultit.
  * Kuutti, K. (1996). Activity theory as a potential framework for human-computer interaction research. In B. A. Nardi (Ed.), *Context and Consciousness: Activity Theory and Human-Computer Interaction* (pp. 17–44). MIT Press.

### Distributed Cognition (dCog)
* **Core Focus:** Posits that human cognition is not contained solely within an individual's brain, but is distributed across people, physical/digital artifacts, visual displays, and surrounding environments.
* **UXR Application:** Vital for studying desk research workflows, collaborative whiteboarding, multi-screen command centers, software switching, and cross-functional team coordination.
* **Key References:**
  * Hollan, J., Hutchins, E., & Kirsh, D. (2000). Distributed cognition: Toward a new foundation for human-computer interaction. *ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction*, 7(2), 174–196. https://doi.org/10.1145/353485.353487

### Grounding Theory
* **Core Focus:** Investigates how conversation participants construct "common ground"—a shared state of mutual understanding, knowledge, and beliefs—through continuous feedback signaling.
* **UXR Application:** Highly applicable to collaborative UX features (comments, co-editing), messaging applications, customer support workflows, and Conversational AI / LLM prompt interactions.
* **Key References:**
  * Clark, H. H., & Brennan, S. E. (1991). Grounding in communication. In L. B. Resnick, J. M. Levine, & S. D. Teasley (Eds.), *Perspectives on Socially Shared Cognition* (pp. 127–149). American Psychological Association.

## 2. Cognitive, Perceptual & Physical Frameworks

### Embodied Cognition & Embodied Interaction
* **Core Focus:** Asserts that human cognition, perception, and meaning-making are deeply grounded in our physical bodily interactions, motor skills, and spatial relationship with the surrounding world.
* **UXR Application:** Essential for spatial computing (AR/VR/XR), haptic devices, mobile gesture interactions, physical hardware interfaces, and tangibly mediated UI design.
* **Key References:**
  * Dourish, P. (2001). *Where the Action Is: The Foundations of Embodied Interaction*. MIT Press.
  * Wilson, M. (2002). Six views of embodied cognition. *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, 9(4), 625–636. https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03196322

### Mental Models Theory
* **Core Focus:** Examines the internal conceptual structure or mental blueprint a user holds regarding how a system operates, based on past experience and inference.
* **UXR Application:** Essential for identifying misalignment between how an engineering architecture works versus how users expect it to work; critical for navigation design, search architecture, and onboarding.
* **Key References:**
  * Craik, K. J. W. (1943). *The Nature of Explanation*. Cambridge University Press.
  * Norman, D. A. (1983). Some observations on mental models. In D. A. Gentner & A. L. Stevens (Eds.), *Mental Models* (pp. 7–14). Lawrence Erlbaum Associates.

### Information Processing & Cognitive Load Theory (CLT)
* **Core Focus:** Models human cognitive architecture in terms of sensory, working, and long-term memory, categorizing mental strain into Intrinsic, Extraneous, and Germane cognitive load.
* **UXR Application:** Evaluates data-dense dashboards, complex decision trees, visual clutter, form flows, and complex UI layouts to prevent user overwhelm and errors.
* **Key References:**
  * Sweller, J. (1988). Cognitive load during problem solving: Effects on learning. *Cognitive Science*, 12(2), 257–285. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4

### Gulf of Execution and Gulf of Evaluation
* **Core Focus:** Analyzes two fundamental gaps in human-system interaction: (1) mapping intentions to allowable actions (Execution), and (2) interpreting system state feedback (Evaluation).
* **UXR Application:** Core framework for identifying interaction friction, ambiguous visual affordances, missing progress indicators, or silent interface errors.
* **Key References:**
  * Norman, D. A. (1986). Cognitive engineering. In D. A. Norman & S. W. Draper (Eds.), *User Centered System Design: New Perspectives on Human-Computer Interaction* (pp. 31–61). Lawrence Erlbaum Associates.

### Fitts’s Law & Keystroke-Level Model (KLM / GOMS)
* **Core Focus:** Provides mathematical and quantitative predictive models of physical movement time, targeting accuracy, visual search, and mechanical task execution speed.
* **UXR Application:** Benchmarking quantitative usability studies, design optimization for micro-interactions, floating action buttons, keyboard shortcuts, and power-user software efficiency.
* **Key References:**
  * Card, S. K., Moran, T. P., & Newell, A. (1980). The Keystroke-Level Model for user performance time with interactive systems. *Communications of the ACM*, 23(7), 396–410. https://doi.org/10.1145/358886.358895
  * Card, S. K., Moran, T. P., & Newell, A. (1983). *The Psychology of Human-Computer Interaction*. Lawrence Erlbaum Associates.

## 3. Behavioral, Motivational & Adoption Frameworks

### Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) & UTAUT
* **Core Focus:** Models individual behavioral intentions to adopt new technology based primarily on *Perceived Usefulness* (PU) and *Perceived Ease of Use* (PEOU), alongside social influence and facilitating conditions.
* **UXR Application:** High-level product-market fit evaluation, enterprise software rollout assessment, initial onboarding acceptance, and feature adoption studies.
* **Key References:**
  * Davis, F. D. (1989). Perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, and user acceptance of information technology. *MIS Quarterly*, 13(3), 319–340. https://doi.org/10.2307/249008
  * Venkatesh, V., Morris, M. G., Davis, G. B., & Davis, F. D. (2003). User acceptance of information technology: Toward a unified view. *MIS Quarterly*, 27(3), 425–478. https://doi.org/10.2307/30036540

### Self-Determination Theory (SDT)
* **Core Focus:** Explores human motivation across a continuum from extrinsic to intrinsic, centered on fulfilling three basic psychological needs: Autonomy, Competence, and Relatedness.
* **UXR Application:** Evaluating long-term user engagement, educational platforms, health/wellness tech, gamification mechanics, and empowering self-service UI flows.
* **Key References:**
  * Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2000). Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being. *American Psychologist*, 55(1), 68–78. https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68

### Sensemaking Theory
* **Core Focus:** Examines how individuals collect, filter, structure, and construct meaningful mental structures out of ambiguous, complex, or messy data environments.
* **UXR Application:** Designing for exploratory search, intelligence synthesis, data visualization tools, diagnostic dashboards, and complex analytical software.
* **Key References:**
  * Dervin, B. (1998). Sense-making theory and practice: An overview of user interests in knowledge seeking and use. *Journal of Knowledge Management*, 2(2), 36–46. https://doi.org/10.1108/13673279810249369
  * Weick, K. E. (1995). *Sensemaking in Organizations*. SAGE Publications.

### Transtheoretical Model / Stages of Change
* **Core Focus:** Maps behavioral transition over time through distinct stages: Precontemplation, Contemplation, Preparation, Action, and Maintenance.
* **UXR Application:** Longitudinal user studies, habit building, behavior change interventions, SaaS activation workflows, and customer retention research.
* **Key References:**
  * Prochaska, J. O., & DiClemente, C. C. (1983). Stages and processes of self-change of smoking: Toward an integrative model of change. *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology*, 51(3), 390–395. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-006X.51.3.390

## 4. Developmental & Pedagogical Frameworks

### Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) & Scaffolding
* **Core Focus:** Identifies the cognitive band between what a user can accomplish unassisted versus what they can achieve with structured assistance, feedback, or scaffolding.
* **UXR Application:** Designing interactive AI coaching, progressive disclosure UI, self-serve onboarding, complex tool mastery, and educational product experiences.
* **Key References:**
  * Vygotsky, L. S. (1978). *Mind in Society: The Development of Higher Psychological Processes*. Harvard University Press.
  * Wood, D., Bruner, J. S., & Ross, G. (1976). The role of tutoring in problem solving. *Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry*, 17(2), 89–100. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.1976.tb00381.x
